## Context paragraph

This project is a machine-vision system that estimates vital signs and stress level from an ordinary RGB video of a person's face, without any contact sensor or extra hardware. It uses remote photoplethysmography (rPPG): each heartbeat causes a tiny, invisible change in skin colour that a camera can measure. MediaPipe FaceLandmarker finds 478 3D face landmarks and the head pose (rotation and translation); the forehead and cheeks are split into about 25 small skin patches defined on the face mesh; the mean R, G, B of each patch is tracked over time; and the POS algorithm turns each patch's colour signal into a candidate pulse signal. Every few seconds each patch receives a trust score from four checks: pulse quality (share of spectral power in the sharpest peak), agreement (weighted vote on the peak frequency across patches), face orientation (patch surface normal from the 3D landmarks) and brightness (shadow or shine). The patch pulses are combined as a trust-weighted average with a confidence value; the part of the signal that follows head movement is removed using the head pose; the breathing rate is estimated from the respiratory modulation of the pulse; the heart rate is read from the FFT peak while rejecting peaks at the breathing rate and its harmonics; and beat detection gives heart-rate variability (RMSSD, SDNN, LF/HF). Finally a stress classifier uses heart rate, HRV and breathing rate, from confident windows only, to output a stress level (low, moderate or high). The system outputs heart rate, breathing rate, HRV, stress level and a confidence value, and reports "signal unreliable" when confidence is low. It runs on a laptop CPU with a normal webcam.
