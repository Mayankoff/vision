## Context paragraph

This project is a machine-vision system that estimates a person's heart rate (in beats per minute, BPM) from an ordinary RGB facial video, without any contact sensor, using remote photoplethysmography (rPPG): each heartbeat causes a tiny, invisible change in skin colour that a camera can measure over time. The system compares three families of methods on the same videos (benchmark datasets UBFC-rPPG, PURE and MMPD, 640x480 at 30 fps) under one shared evaluation protocol. (1) Classical methods: MediaPipe FaceLandmarker finds 478 3D face landmarks; three skin regions (forehead, left cheek, right cheek) are masked; their mean R, G, B values per frame form a colour trace; a signal-processing method (GREEN, ICA, CHROM or POS) turns that trace into a pulse waveform. (2) Deep-learning methods from rPPG-Toolbox: a Haar-cascade face box (enlarged 1.5x) is cropped and resized to 72x72 or 128x128 frames, and a neural network (PhysNet, TS-CAN, EfficientPhys or PhysFormer) predicts the pulse waveform directly from the video clip. (3) The proposed method, designed in this project: "quality-aware, motion-compensated multi-patch rPPG". It splits the forehead and cheeks into about 25 small patches defined on the MediaPipe face mesh; computes the mean RGB of each patch in every frame; applies POS separately to each patch, producing 25 candidate pulse signals; and every few seconds gives each patch a trust score from four checks: pulse quality (the share of the patch pulse's spectral power in its sharpest peak), agreement (a weighted vote on the peak frequency across all patches; patches that disagree are down-weighted), face orientation (the patch's surface normal from the 3D landmarks; patches turned away from the camera are down-weighted) and brightness (patches too dark from shadow or too bright from shine are down-weighted). The patch pulses are combined as a trust-weighted average, then motion compensation subtracts the part of the signal that follows head movement, using the head pose (rotation and translation) from MediaPipe as a reference. All three branches end in the same post-processing: detrending, band-pass filtering at 0.7-3.0 Hz (42-180 BPM) and an FFT whose highest peak gives the heart rate. The proposed method additionally outputs a confidence value and can report "signal unreliable" when trust is low. Results are evaluated with MAE, RMSE, MAPE, Pearson correlation and SNR, broken down by MMPD condition (skin tone, motion, lighting), together with processing time per frame. The proposed method needs no training and runs in real time on a laptop CPU.
