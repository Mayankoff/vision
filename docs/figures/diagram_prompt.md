## Context paragraph

This project is a machine-vision system that screens for atrial fibrillation (AF, an irregular heart rhythm that is often silent and raises stroke risk up to five-fold) and flags slow, fast or extra heartbeats from an ordinary RGB video of a person's face, without any contact sensor or extra hardware. It uses remote photoplethysmography (rPPG): each heartbeat causes a tiny, invisible change in skin colour that a camera can measure. MediaPipe FaceLandmarker finds 478 3D face landmarks and the head pose (rotation and translation); the forehead and cheeks are split into about 25 small skin patches defined on the face mesh; the mean R, G, B of each patch is tracked over time; and the POS algorithm turns each patch's colour signal into a candidate pulse signal. Every few seconds each patch receives a trust score from four checks: pulse quality (share of spectral power in the sharpest peak), agreement (weighted vote on the peak frequency across patches), face orientation (patch surface normal from the 3D landmarks) and brightness (shadow or shine). The patch pulses are combined as a trust-weighted average with a confidence value; the part of the signal that follows head movement is removed using the head pose; the breathing rate is estimated from the respiratory modulation of the pulse; the heart rate is read from the FFT peak while rejecting peaks at the breathing rate and its harmonics; and beat detection gives inter-beat intervals and heart-rate variability (RMSSD, SDNN, LF/HF). The primary analysis is AF screening: rhythm-irregularity features of the inter-beat intervals (RMSSD, entropy, Poincare plot spread), from confident windows only, are classified as regular rhythm or possible AF. Motion and lighting normally create false AF alarms, which the trust scores and confidence gating are designed to prevent. The secondary analysis flags heart-rate abnormalities from the same data: a slow (below 50 BPM) or fast (above 100 BPM) resting heart rate lasting at least 2 minutes, and extra (premature) beats, which are also removed before the AF check because they can mimic AF. The system outputs heart rate, breathing rate, HRV, the rhythm result ("regular" or "possible AF - see a doctor"), the heart-rate flags (normal, slow or fast; extra beats) and a confidence value, and reports "signal unreliable" when confidence is low. It runs on a laptop CPU with a normal webcam.
