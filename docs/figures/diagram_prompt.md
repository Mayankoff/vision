## Context paragraph

This project is a machine-vision system that screens for atrial fibrillation (AF, an irregular heart rhythm that is often silent and raises stroke risk up to five-fold) and flags slow, fast or extra heartbeats from an ordinary RGB video of a person's face, without any contact sensor or extra hardware. It uses remote photoplethysmography (rPPG): each heartbeat causes a tiny, invisible change in skin colour that a camera can measure. MediaPipe FaceLandmarker finds 478 3D face landmarks and the head pose (rotation and translation); the forehead and cheeks are split into 6 to 25 small skin patches defined on the face mesh (more when the face is large in the frame); the mean R, G, B of each patch is tracked over time; and the POS algorithm turns each patch's colour signal into a candidate pulse signal. Every second each patch receives a trust score from four checks that do not assume a regular rhythm (so real AF is not mistaken for noise): face orientation (patch surface normal from the 3D landmarks), brightness (shadow or shine), patch consistency (correlation with the other patches' pulse) and beat agreement (do the patch's beats line up with the consensus beats). The patch pulses are combined as a trust-weighted average with a confidence value (from patch consistency, beat-shape consistency, head motion and the number of trusted patches); the part of the signal that follows head movement is removed by ridge regression on the head pose; the breathing rate is estimated from the respiratory modulation of the pulse; the heart rate is read from the FFT peak while rejecting peaks at the breathing rate and its harmonics; and beat detection gives inter-beat intervals and heart-rate variability (RMSSD, SDNN). The primary analysis is AF screening: rhythm-irregularity features of the inter-beat intervals (normalised RMSSD, entropy, Poincare plot spread, turning-point ratio), from confident windows only, are classified as regular rhythm or possible AF. Motion and lighting normally create false AF alarms, which the trust scores and confidence gating are designed to prevent. The secondary analysis flags heart-rate abnormalities from the same data: a slow (below 50 BPM) or fast (above 100 BPM) resting heart rate lasting at least 2 minutes, and extra (premature) beats, which are also removed before the AF check because they can mimic AF. The system outputs heart rate, breathing rate, HRV, the rhythm result ("regular" or "possible AF - see a doctor"), the heart-rate flags (normal, slow or fast; extra beats) and a confidence value, and reports "signal unreliable" when confidence is low. It runs on a laptop CPU with a normal webcam.
